<SectionHeading id="why-now">Why 2026 Is the Right Time</SectionHeading>

The property preservation industry is counter-cyclical. When the economy softens and mortgage delinquencies rise, preservation volume explodes. When times are good, insurance inspections and preventive maintenance keep the baseline healthy. Either way, someone has to physically visit properties.

What makes 2026 particularly compelling is the convergence of three forces: rising interest rates pushing more homeowners into delinquency, aging housing stock requiring more frequent condition monitoring, and a wave of retiring baby-boomer contractors leaving territories wide open.

If you have been doing field inspections as a solo 1099 contractor and want to build something bigger, this is your playbook.

<Callout type="tip" title="The Income Ceiling Problem">
As a solo inspector, your income is capped by the number of hours in a day and the number of orders you can physically complete. The only way to break through that ceiling is to build a business that generates revenue even when you are not personally in the field.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="legal-foundation">Legal Foundation</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Form Your LLC">
File with your state's Secretary of State. Cost varies from $50 (Kentucky) to $500 (Massachusetts). Choose a professional name — avoid anything cute. Asset managers want to see names like "Pinnacle Field Services LLC" not "Bob's Handy Inspections". Get your EIN from the IRS (free, takes 5 minutes online).
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Open a Business Bank Account">
Separate your business finances from day one. This is non-negotiable for tax purposes and professional credibility. Most banks offer free business checking for LLCs with low balances.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Register with Your State Tax Authority">
Depending on your state, you may need to register for sales tax collection on certain preservation services. Consult a local CPA — a one-hour consultation now prevents an audit nightmare later.
</StepCard>

<SectionHeading id="insurance">Insurance Requirements</SectionHeading>

Insurance is the single biggest barrier separating solo inspectors from legitimate preservation businesses. National companies will not assign orders to uninsured vendors.

<RequirementsGrid>
  <RequirementCard>General Liability Insurance — $1,000,000 minimum per occurrence</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Errors and Omissions (E&O) — protects against reporting mistakes</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Workers Compensation — required in most states if you hire employees</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Commercial Auto Insurance — your personal policy does not cover business use</RequirementCard>
</RequirementsGrid>

<Callout type="warning" title="The COI Game">
Every national company will demand a Certificate of Insurance (COI) naming them as an Additional Insured. Your insurance provider will issue these for free or a small fee. You will send dozens of COIs throughout the year. Build a template folder and keep your policy documents organized.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="equipment">Equipment and Tools</SectionHeading>

You do not need a $50,000 truck and a warehouse full of equipment to start. Here is what you actually need on day one versus what you can add as revenue grows.

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="Day One Essentials (Under $500)">
    Padlock set (keyed-alike), lockbox, cordless drill with bit set, tape measure, flashlight, basic hand tools, trash bags, broom, and a quality smartphone mount for your vehicle. Everything fits in the trunk of a sedan.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Month Two Upgrades ($500 to $1,500)">
    Winterization kit (air compressor, antifreeze, pipe caps), lawn mower (if doing grass cuts), plywood sheets and boarding hardware, and a cargo carrier or small trailer.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Growth Phase ($2,000+)">
    Cargo van or pickup truck, commercial-grade lawn equipment, pressure washer, generator, and bulk supply storage at home or in a small unit.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

<SectionHeading id="first-contracts">Landing Your First Contracts</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Join Every National Panel">
Apply to Safeguard, MCS, Cyprexx, NFR, Five Brothers, and ServiceLink simultaneously. Each application takes 20 to 40 minutes. Include your LLC documentation, insurance COIs, W-9, and a professional cover letter.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Target Regional Companies in Your State">
Regional firms pay 15 to 40 percent more per order than nationals. Use our directory to filter by your state and apply to every regional firm that covers your territory. These are your margin builders.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Deliver Flawlessly for 90 Days">
Your first 90 days define your vendor reputation. Every order on time. Every photo set complete. Every report accurate. Companies assign more volume to reliable contractors and cut unreliable ones without warning.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={4} title="Ask for More Territory">
After proving yourself, actively request expanded zip codes. If you cover a 30-mile radius today, ask to extend to 50. More territory means more orders means more revenue.
</StepCard>

<SectionHeading id="scaling">Scaling Beyond Solo</SectionHeading>

The transition from solo contractor to business owner happens when you start hiring subcontractors to complete orders on your behalf. You become the manager, not the inspector.

<TipStack>
  <TipCard title="Hire Your First Sub at $3,000 Monthly Revenue">
    When you are consistently earning $3,000 or more per month from a single company, you have enough volume to train a subcontractor on that company's specific requirements and hand off orders while retaining a margin.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Keep 20 to 30 Percent of Each Order">
    If a national pays you $50 for a winterization, pay your sub $35 to $40 and keep the difference. Your sub gets reliable work. You get passive income on orders you are not physically completing.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Quality Control is Everything">
    One bad subcontractor can destroy your vendor account permanently. Spot-check their work. Review their photos before submission. Fire fast if quality drops.
  </TipCard>
</TipStack>
