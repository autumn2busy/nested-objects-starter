<SectionHeading id="why-compliance">Why Compliance Matters</SectionHeading>

You found a great company, filled out the application, and got excited about starting work. Then you hit the compliance wall: a 47-field form asking for documents you have never heard of, a background check that takes two weeks, and an insurance requirement you did not budget for.

This is where most new inspectors give up. They apply to one company, get overwhelmed by the compliance process, and never finish onboarding. Meanwhile, the inspectors who have their compliance packet pre-built are getting approved in 5 days and already completing orders.

Compliance exists because banks and lenders require every contractor who touches their properties to be vetted. The field services company is legally liable for your actions on their client's assets. They need to verify you are who you say you are, you have no disqualifying criminal history, and you carry insurance in case something goes wrong.

<Callout type="tip" title="Build Your Compliance Packet Once">
Create a single folder on your phone and computer containing every document listed below. When you apply to a new company, you will be able to upload everything in under 10 minutes instead of scrambling to find documents over multiple days.
</Callout>

Every legitimate field services company will request these core documents during onboarding. No exceptions.

<RequirementsGrid>

<SectionHeading id="background">Background Check Details</SectionHeading>

The background check is the most anxiety-inducing part of compliance for new inspectors. Here is exactly what most companies screen for.

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="Criminal History (7-Year Lookback)">
    Most companies run a 7-year criminal history check at the county, state, and federal level. Felony convictions — especially burglary, theft, fraud, and drug trafficking — are typically automatic disqualifiers. Misdemeanors are evaluated case by case.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Sex Offender Registry">
    All major field services companies check the national sex offender registry. Registration on any state's list is an automatic and permanent disqualifier.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Federal Exclusion Lists">
    Companies check OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control), GSA (General Services Administration), and other federal exclusion databases. Being on any federal exclusion list blocks you from working on government-backed mortgage assets.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Credit Check (Rare)">
    Some companies, particularly those offering direct bank contracts, may run a credit check. This is uncommon for standard subcontractor roles but does occur for high-trust REO asset management positions.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

<SectionHeading id="insurance-docs">Insurance Documentation</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="General Liability Insurance">
Minimum $500,000 per occurrence for basic mortgage inspections. Most nationals require $1,000,000 per occurrence. You must provide a Certificate of Insurance (COI) naming the company as an Additional Insured. Expect to pay $800 to $2,000 annually depending on your state and coverage limits.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Errors and Omissions (E&O)">
Required by many companies, especially for interior inspections and REO work. E&O protects you and the company if your inspection report contains errors that lead to financial loss. Typical cost: $300 to $800 annually.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Workers Compensation">
Required in most states if you hire any employees or subcontractors. Some companies require it even for solo operators depending on state law. Check your state's requirements before assuming you are exempt.
</StepCard>

<SectionHeading id="company-specific">Company-Specific Requirements</SectionHeading>

Beyond the universal requirements, individual companies may have additional screening steps.

<TipStack>
  <TipCard title="Company-Specific Training Modules">
    Many nationals require you to complete their proprietary online training course before activating your account. These typically take 1 to 4 hours and cover their specific photo requirements, reporting standards, and order acceptance protocols.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Equipment Verification">
    Some preservation companies require photo proof that you own specific tools — drill, lock sets, winterization equipment. They want to verify you can actually complete the work before sending orders.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Territory Verification">
    Companies verify that you actually live within or near the territory you are requesting. They may ask for a utility bill or lease agreement showing your physical address within the coverage area.
  </TipCard>
</TipStack>

<SectionHeading id="pro-tips">Pro Tips for Fast Onboarding</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Apply to 5 Companies on the Same Day">
Since background checks take 5 to 15 days regardless, starting 5 applications simultaneously means all 5 approvals land within the same window. You go from zero companies to five in two weeks instead of two months.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Pre-Fill Your W-9">
Download the W-9 from the IRS website, fill it out completely, sign it, and save a clean PDF. You will upload this exact file to every single company you apply to.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Get Insurance Before Applying">
Do not wait until a company asks for your COI. Get your general liability policy set up first. When the compliance form asks for your insurance information, you submit it immediately instead of pausing the process for a week.
</StepCard>
