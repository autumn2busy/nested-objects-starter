## Key Takeaways
- **You are capped by geography:** A solo inspector can only drive so many miles in a day. Scaling requires leveraging other people's time.
- **Compliance is the bottleneck:** You must ensure your subs maintain the same level of quality and compliance, or your master contract will be terminated.
- **Workflow Automation:** You cannot manually assign jobs via text message if you want to surpass 500 work orders a month.

<h2 id="the-ceiling">The Ceiling of a Solo Inspector</h2>

Every successful independent field inspector eventually hits a wall. No matter how perfectly you optimize your route, there are only so many hours of daylight to take photos, and only so many hours at night to upload them to Pruvan or EZInspections. 

To cross the six-figure threshold into seven figures, you must transition from an "inspector" to a "managing vendor."

<h2 id="building-crews">Building W2 vs 1099 Crews</h2>

When expanding your territory to cover an entire state, you have two choices for staffing:

1.  **1099 Sub-Contractors:** Lower overhead, but lower control. You take a 20-30% cut of the work order and pass the rest to the sub. The risk? If they submit blurry photos, *you* get the penalty fee.
2.  **W2 Employees:** High overhead, high control. You buy the fleet vehicles and pay hourly. This is the model required for heavy property preservation (roof tarping, evictions) where liability is incredibly high.

<h2 id="software">The Software Tech Stack</h2>

You can no longer manage your routes on a Google Sheet. Regional providers in 2026 rely on a dedicated tech stack:
-   **Routing:** Circuit or Route4Me to aggressively dense-pack your drivers' zones.
-   **Field App:** Using white-labeled apps to force your crews to take geo-stamped photos *before* they can submit the job.
-   **Invoicing:** Automating your accounting so you don't spend Sundays tracking down missing $15 checks.
