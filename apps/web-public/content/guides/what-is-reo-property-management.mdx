<SectionHeading id="what-is-reo">What is REO?</SectionHeading>

When a homeowner defaults on their mortgage and the property fails to sell at a foreclosure auction, the lending institution takes ownership of that property. At that exact moment, the house transitions from a "delinquent mortgage asset" to an "REO asset" — Real Estate Owned by the bank.

The bank does not want to own houses. Banks are financial institutions, not property management companies. But until that property is repaired, listed, and sold to a new buyer, someone has to physically manage it. That someone is an REO contractor.

This is where the money gets serious for field inspectors who are ready to graduate beyond basic drive-by occupancy checks.

<Callout type="info" title="Scale of the REO Market">
At any given time, major banks like Wells Fargo, JPMorgan Chase, and Bank of America collectively hold tens of thousands of REO properties across the country. Each one requires regular inspections, maintenance, and preservation until disposal. This is a permanent, structurally embedded market.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="lifecycle">The REO Property Lifecycle</SectionHeading>

Understanding the lifecycle helps you identify exactly where your services fit and which stage pays the most.

<StepCard number={1} title="Foreclosure Completes">
The legal process concludes and the bank takes title. The property is now bank-owned. An initial securing order is immediately generated — this is the highest-urgency, highest-paying moment in the lifecycle.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Initial Securing and Winterization">
The property must be secured against vandalism, weather damage, and liability. Lock changes, boarding, pipe draining, and hazard remediation happen here. This is preservation-level work paying $50 to $200+ per order.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Ongoing Maintenance">
While the property sits on the market, it needs regular lawn care, debris removal, snow removal, and condition monitoring. These are recurring orders — the same property generates revenue for you every 2 to 4 weeks.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={4} title="Pre-Sale Preparation">
Before listing, the bank orders full interior condition reports, repair estimates, and sometimes cosmetic improvements. Detailed photo documentation and measurement work is required here.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={5} title="Disposal">
The property sells. Your recurring revenue from that address stops, but the bank already has 50 more properties cycling into REO status behind it.
</StepCard>

<SectionHeading id="your-role">Your Role as an REO Inspector</SectionHeading>

REO work is fundamentally different from standard mortgage inspections. You are not just snapping three photos from the curb. You are acting as the bank's physical representative for a property they own.

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="Condition Reporting">
    Detailed interior and exterior documentation. Every room, every deficiency, every code violation. Asset managers use your reports to make repair-or-sell decisions worth tens of thousands of dollars.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Vendor Coordination">
    In many REO contracts, you are responsible for coordinating third-party vendors — locksmiths, lawn crews, plumbers, electricians. You become the project manager on the ground.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Occupancy Enforcement">
    Squatters in REO properties are a massive liability. You monitor for unauthorized occupancy, document evidence, and trigger the legal removal process through the asset manager.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Cash-for-Keys Negotiation">
    Some REO contracts require you to negotiate with remaining occupants. You offer a cash incentive for them to vacate voluntarily instead of going through costly eviction proceedings.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

<SectionHeading id="who-hires">Who Hires REO Contractors</SectionHeading>

<TipStack>
  <TipCard title="National Preservation Companies">
    Safeguard Properties, MCS, Cyprexx, and Five Brothers all have massive REO preservation divisions. They subcontract to local inspectors at standardized rates. High volume, moderate pay per order.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Regional Asset Management Firms">
    Smaller firms that manage REO portfolios for regional banks and credit unions. They typically pay better than nationals and give you more autonomy. These are your best targets for long-term relationships.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Real Estate Brokerages with REO Divisions">
    Some RE/MAX, Keller Williams, and Coldwell Banker offices manage REO listings for banks. They need reliable inspectors for pre-listing condition reports. Excellent networking opportunity.
  </TipCard>
</TipStack>

<SectionHeading id="breaking-in">How to Break Into REO Work</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Build your base with mortgage inspections first">
Complete 90+ days of clean mortgage inspection work. REO companies want contractors with proven track records, not complete beginners. Your reliability score matters.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Get preservation-capable">
Invest in basic tools — lock sets, winterization supplies, a drill. REO work blurs the line between inspection and preservation. The contractors who can do both get significantly more volume and higher pay.
</StepCard>
