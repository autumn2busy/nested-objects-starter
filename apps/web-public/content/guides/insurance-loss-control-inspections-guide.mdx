<SectionHeading id="what-is-loss-control">What is Loss Control?</SectionHeading>

If you've proven yourself in the standard mortgage field inspection space and are looking to level-up your per-order payout, **Insurance Loss Control** is the premier target. 

Unlike a basic occupancy check where you merely take three photos from your car indicating whether the lawn looks mowed, a Loss Control inspection involves comprehensive risk-assessment logic. Insurance carriers—firms like Liberty Mutual, Travelers, and Allstate—must confirm exactly what they are legally insuring prior to locking in an underwriter's binding policy. 

They sub-contract these assessment evaluations out to specialized Field Inspection companies. 

<Callout type="warning" title="Accuracy is Paramount">
In standard mortgage preservation, you are maintaining a defaulted asset. In loss control, you are setting the financial perimeter for a million-dollar liability contract. Missing a frayed fusebox or an aggressive dog breed on the premises can result in immediate termination of your vendor contract.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="role">The Role of the Inspector</SectionHeading>

When assigned an order, you will schedule an appointment with the actual homeowner or business owner (unlike REO and foreclosure work, these properties are occupied and functional). 

Your duties revolve around identifying factors that could cause a future claim. 

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="The Identification of Hazards">
    You will look for liability hazards like broken sidewalks, missing handrails, lack of smoke detectors, exposed wiring, and severe structural degradation.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Valuation and Measurement">
    You will frequently be required to verify the square footage footprint of the property. This involves physically walking the perimeter of the housing structure with a measuring wheel or using satellite CAD technologies to map out exact replacements costs.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Life Safety Protocols">
    For commercial inspections, you will check for fire-suppression systems, sprinkler certifications, safe-clearances for commercial kitchen hoods, and egress pathways.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

<SectionHeading id="residential-vs-commercial">Residential vs Commercial</SectionHeading>

The space diverges heavily into two unique verticals. 

| Tier | Characteristics | Typical Payout |
|---|---|---|
| **Residential (High-Value Dwelling)** | Interior/Exterior diagrams, roof conditions, general hazard identification. Appointments required. | $40 - $90 |
| **Commercial Risk Surveys** | Deep-dive evaluations into restaurant suppression systems, fleet auto-garages, manufacturing hazards. Requires interviews. | $150 - $400+ |

<SectionHeading id="tools">Core Tools & Tech</SectionHeading>

Because loss control pays exponentially higher than drive-by mortgage orders, the required toolkit is slightly more specific. Most contractors invest heavily into tools that make measuring faster so they can execute more orders per day.

<StepCard number={1} title="The Measuring Wheel">
A standard D-tape or a mechanical measuring wheel is the foundational tool. Some modern inspectors upgrade to laser measurement devices to calculate complex roof or vaulted interior parameters safely.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Graph Paper / Diagram Software">
You have to deliver a readable footprint sketch. Modern inspection apps built for loss control often have integrated sketch-pads, but many veterans prefer to diagram physically on site and digitize later.
</StepCard>
