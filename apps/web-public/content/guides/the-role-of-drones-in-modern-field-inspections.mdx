<SectionHeading id="the-aerial-shift">The Aerial Shift</SectionHeading>

The field services industry has reached a tipping point. For decades, inspecting a roof meant climbing a ladder, risking a fall, and potentially damaging fragile shingles. In 2026, the ladder is increasingly staying on the truck.

Drone technology is no longer a "cool gadget" for field inspectors—it is becoming a standard operational requirement for high-margin contracts. Aerial data provides a perspective that ground-level photography simply cannot match, especially for multi-story properties, large commercial complexes, and estate-sized residential lots.

<Callout type="info" title="The Productivity Boost">
An experienced drone pilot can capture every mandatory roof angle, chimney detail, and gutter line in under 4 minutes. A manual roof walk with a ladder setup takes 20 to 30 minutes. Drones are not just safer—they are an efficiency multiplier.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="part-107">FAA Part 107 Compliance</SectionHeading>

Before you buy a drone and start charging for photos, you must understand the legal landscape. The FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) draws a strict line between recreational hobbyists and commercial pilots.

<RequirementsGrid>
  <RequirementCard>FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate (Mandatory for business).</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Drone Registration (Every drone over 250g must be registered).</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Remote ID Compliance (Most modern drones have this built-in).</RequirementCard>
  <RequirementCard>Commercial Drone Insurance (LAANC-integrated policies like SkyWatch).</RequirementCard>
</RequirementsGrid>

<SectionHeading id="workflow">Adding Drones to Your Workflow</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Initial Site Scan">
Upon arrival, perform a quick 360-degree aerial orbit. This establishes the property context and identifies any large-scale hazards (missing shingles, fallen trees, adjacent vacant lots) immediately.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="The 5-Photo Roof Set">
Most companies require a top-down "orthomosaic" view plus 4 isometric angles. A drone executes this sequence in seconds. Ensure your camera is tilted at 45 degrees for the isometric shots.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Detailed Asset Monitoring">
Use the high-resolution zoom to document specific deficiencies: chimney flashing, valley debris, and gutter clogs. These high-quality photos drastically reduce the "request for more info" (rereads) from asset managers.
</StepCard>
