<SectionHeading id="what-is">What are mortgage field services?</SectionHeading>

Mortgage field services is the industry that inspects, preserves, and maintains properties on behalf of mortgage lenders. Every time a homeowner falls behind on their mortgage — or a property goes into foreclosure — the lender needs someone to physically visit that property and document what's happening.

That someone is a mortgage field inspector.

By federal law, when a mortgage goes more than 45 days delinquent, the lender is required to generate a work order to inspect the property. This creates a constant, legally mandated demand for field inspectors across all 50 states. As long as mortgages exist, this work exists.

The industry is massive but invisible to most people. Hundreds of companies employ tens of thousands of independent contractors to perform millions of inspections every year. And most of those contractors found the work through word of mouth — because nobody talks about it.

<SectionHeading id="how-it-works">How the industry works</SectionHeading>

The supply chain has clear layers:

<StepCard number={1} title="Banks and loan servicers (the clients)">
  JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, Mr. Cooper, Nationstar — these are the companies that own or service mortgages. They need properties inspected but don't employ inspectors directly.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="National field services companies (the primes)">
  Companies like Safeguard Properties, NFR, ServiceLink, and MCS hold contracts with the banks. They receive thousands of work orders per day and distribute them across the country.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Regional field services companies (the subs)">
  Many nationals subcontract to regional companies that cover specific states or metro areas. Regionals often pay better per order because they have lower overhead.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={4} title="You — the independent contractor">
  You sign up with nationals, regionals, or both. You receive work orders via mobile app, complete the inspection, submit your report and photos, and get paid per order.
</StepCard>

<Callout type="tip" title="Why this matters to you">
  Understanding the supply chain helps you negotiate. If a national is paying you $8 per inspection, the bank is paying them $15–$25 for that same order. Regionals sit closer to the money and often pass more of it to you.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="roles">Roles in mortgage field services</SectionHeading>

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="Occupancy Inspector">
    The entry point. You drive to a property, verify whether it's occupied or vacant, take required photos, and submit via app. Fast, high-volume work. No interior access needed.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Property Preservation Contractor">
    You secure, winterize, and maintain vacant properties. Lock changes, boarding, pipe draining, debris removal, lawn care. Requires basic tools and handyman skills. Pays significantly more per order.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Insurance Loss Inspector">
    You evaluate properties for insurance underwriting or claims. Interior and exterior inspection with detailed documentation. Highest pay in the field inspection world ($50–$150+ per inspection).
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="REO Asset Manager / Inspector">
    You manage bank-owned properties after foreclosure — coordinating repairs, maintaining condition, and preparing for sale. More responsibility and more pay.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Quality Control Inspector">
    You re-inspect work done by other contractors to verify completion and quality. Companies pay well for QC because bad work costs them money.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

Most people start as occupancy inspectors and branch into preservation or insurance work within 3–6 months.

<SectionHeading id="getting-started">How to get started</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Decide if this is right for you">
  This is independent contractor work. No guaranteed hours, no benefits, no boss telling you what to do. If you want flexibility and are comfortable with variable income, keep reading.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Get the basics in order">
  You need a reliable vehicle, a smartphone with a decent camera, internet access, and a clean background. That's the barrier to entry — it's intentionally low.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Complete a training course">
  A structured training course teaches you inspection types, photo requirements, reporting standards, and how to avoid common mistakes that get new inspectors rejected. Our training tracks are built specifically for this.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={4} title="Apply to multiple companies">
  Don't apply to one company and wait. Apply to 3–5 simultaneously. Use our directory to find companies that serve your state, compare pay data, and apply directly.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={5} title="Complete your first 30 days clean">
  Your first month is a trial period. Every report on time, every photo set complete, every communication professional. After 30 clean days, you'll start getting more volume and better orders.
</StepCard>
