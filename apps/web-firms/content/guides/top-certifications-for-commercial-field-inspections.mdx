## Key Takeaways
- **Higher Pay:** Commercial inspections pay 5-10x more than residential foreclosure checks.
- **Barrier to Entry:** Unlike residential, commercial vendors actually verify your credentials before onboarding you.
- **OSHA Matters:** Basic safety and hazard recognition is the #1 skill insurance companies look for.

<h2 id="why-certify">Why Certify?</h2>

The residential mortgage field service industry has a famously low barrier to entry. Anyone with a smartphone and a car can do a drive-by eviction check.

However, the **commercial sector** evaluates multi-million dollar assets, active construction zones, and high-liability leased equipment. When a firm deploys you to inspect a strip mall's roof for an insurance underwriter, they need to know you won't fall off and sue them. 

<h2 id="top-certs">Top 3 Commercial Certifications</h2>

1. **OSHA 10 or OSHA 30 (General Industry or Construction)**
   - *Why you need it:* Nearly all commercial loss control gigs require you to spot safety hazards (blocked fire exits, trip hazards, lack of PPE). You cannot spot them if you aren't trained in them.
2. **NFPA Life Safety Code Certification**
   - *Why you need it:* Fire safety is the biggest risk for commercial property underwriters. Knowing how sprinkler systems and fire doors function makes you incredibly valuable to loss control firms.
3. **Drone Pilot (Part 107) License**
   - *Why you need it:* Commercial roofs are often inaccessible or too dangerous to walk. Having an FAA-certified drone license allows you to charge premium rates for aerial commercial damage assessments.

<h2 id="getting-hired">How to Market Your Credentials</h2>

Once you obtain your certifications, do not just casually list them on your resume. 

Add the official logos to your business profile on the Nested Objects directory, and explicitly state in your outreach emails that you are "OSHA 30 Certified and FAA Part 107 Licensed."

Commercial hiring managers search specifically for these terms when deploying vendors for high-value jobs.
