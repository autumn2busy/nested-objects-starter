<SectionHeading id="overview">The real numbers — no sugarcoating</SectionHeading>

Let's cut to it. The internet is full of vague "field inspectors can make $100/hour!" claims. Some of that is technically possible. But most new contractors start at the lower end, and your actual income depends entirely on what types of work you take, how many companies you work with, and how dense your territory is.

The national average salary for a property preservation contractor is roughly $61,000 per year according to Glassdoor and ZipRecruiter data. But that number hides a massive range — from $30,000 for part-timers doing drive-bys to $80,000+ for experienced contractors stacking preservation orders across multiple firms.

Here's what actually matters: **pay per order, orders per day, and days per week.**

<SectionHeading id="by-type">Pay by inspection type</SectionHeading>

| Order Type | Typical Pay | Time Required | Skill Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exterior / Drive-by | $5 – $15 | 5–10 min | Entry level |
| Occupancy Verification | $10 – $25 | 10–20 min | Entry level |
| Door Knock + Letter Post | $8 – $18 | 10–15 min | Entry level |
| Interior Inspection | $25 – $75 | 30–60 min | Intermediate |
| Property Securing (lock change) | $25 – $60 | 20–40 min | Intermediate |
| Winterization | $50 – $150 | 1–2 hours | Intermediate |
| Debris Removal (per cubic yard) | $25 – $45/yd | Varies | Intermediate |
| Lawn / Maintenance | $25 – $65 | 30–60 min | Entry level |
| Insurance Loss Control | $50 – $150 | 45–90 min | Advanced |
| Commercial Inspection | $75 – $200+ | 1–3 hours | Advanced |

<Callout type="tip" title="The volume vs. value tradeoff">
  Drive-by inspections pay $5–$15 each, but an efficient inspector can complete 15–20 per day on a good route. That's $75–$300/day. Interior inspections pay $25–$75 each, but you'll only complete 4–8 per day. The math often works out similarly — the question is which work style suits you.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="by-tier">National vs regional companies</SectionHeading>

<InfoStack>
  <InfoBlock title="National companies (Safeguard, NFR, MCS, Cyprexx, ServiceLink)">
    Higher volume, more consistent orders. Pay tends to be standardized and slightly lower per order. But you'll rarely run out of work. Good for building a base income.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Regional companies (state-specific or multi-state firms)">
    Often pay 10–30% more per order than nationals. Lower volume but better margins. Many are listed in our directory — filter by your state to find them.
  </InfoBlock>
  <InfoBlock title="Direct bank/lender contracts">
    The highest pay per order. Harder to get. Usually requires a track record and sometimes an LLC or insurance. This is the endgame for experienced contractors.
  </InfoBlock>
</InfoStack>

The smart play is to stack 2–3 nationals for base volume, then layer in 1–2 regionals for higher-paying orders.

<SectionHeading id="factors">What affects your pay</SectionHeading>

<TipStack>
  <TipCard title="Territory density">
    Urban and suburban territories have more properties per square mile. Less driving = more inspections per hour = higher effective hourly rate.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Number of companies you work for">
    Working for one company limits your volume. Working for 3–5 means your phone is always buzzing with orders. More orders = more income.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Order type mix">
    A contractor doing only drive-bys at $8 each is in a very different position than one doing 50% interior inspections at $50 each. Push toward higher-value order types as you gain experience.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Speed and accuracy">
    Companies assign more volume to fast, accurate inspectors. If you're consistently completing orders same-day with clean reports, you'll get priority on new assignments.
  </TipCard>
  <TipCard title="Seasonality">
    Property preservation volume spikes in fall (winterization) and spring (maintenance). Mortgage inspections are steadier year-round. Plan for seasonal dips.
  </TipCard>
</TipStack>

<Callout type="info" title="These are gross numbers">
  Remember — as a 1099 contractor, you're responsible for self-employment tax (~15.3%), fuel, vehicle wear, and other expenses. Net take-home is typically 60–75% of gross, depending on your territory and driving distance.
</Callout>

<SectionHeading id="maximize">How to maximize your earnings</SectionHeading>

<StepCard number={1} title="Sign up with 3–5 companies immediately">
  Don't wait until one company is slow to apply to others. Get on multiple panels from day one. Our directory lists 460+ firms with pay ranges and apply links.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={2} title="Optimize your route">
  Batch inspections by geography. Use route optimization apps. The difference between a random route and an optimized one can be 2–3 extra inspections per day.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={3} title="Move up the value chain">
  Start with exterior inspections to learn the workflow, then push for interior inspections, preservation orders, and eventually insurance loss control. Each step up significantly increases pay per order.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={4} title="Negotiate after proving yourself">
  After 60–90 days of clean, on-time work, ask your contact for higher-paying order types or expanded territory. Most companies won't offer — you have to ask.
</StepCard>

<StepCard number={5} title="Track everything">
  Mileage, expenses, time per inspection. Know your effective hourly rate by company and order type. Drop companies that aren't worth your time. Double down on the ones that are.
</StepCard>

The IRS standard mileage rate for 2026 is your friend. If you're driving 1,000+ miles per week for inspections, the mileage deduction alone can offset a significant chunk of your tax liability. Track from day one — apps like Everlance or Stride do it automatically.
