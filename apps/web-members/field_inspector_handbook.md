# The Field Inspector's Handbook
## Chapter 1: Orientation & Basics

### Introduction to Field Inspections
Field inspections are the eyes and ears of the mortgage and insurance industries. Banks and insurers cannot physically visit every property they manage; instead, they rely on independent field inspectors to verify the condition and occupancy status of residential properties. Your job is to be an objective observer, capturing factual data and photographs without offering opinions or advice to homeowners.

### Essential Tools & Safety
Before you start your car, ensure you have the "Holy Trinity" of inspection tools:
1.  **Smartphone with a high-quality camera**: Your photos are your product.
2.  **Backup Battery/Charger**: inspection apps drain battery famously fast.
3.  **ID Badge**: Always worn visibly on a lanyard.

**Safety First**:
*   **Situational Awareness**: Never enter a backyard if you can't see the fence line (dogs).
*   **De-escalation**: If a homeowner is hostile, leave immediately. Do not argue. "I'm just snapping a few photos for the mortgage company" is your go-to phrase.
*   **Driving**: You will be driving *a lot*. Maintain your vehicle.

### Ethics & Conduct
*   **No Contact**: Unless specified, do not knock. Most checks are "exterior only".
*   **Honesty**: Never "drive-by" a photo. You must be at the address. GPS metadata tracks you.
*   **Privacy**: Blur license plates and faces of people in your photos accidentally.

## Chapter 2: Core Inspections

### Occupancy Verification
The most common inspection type. The bank basically wants to know: "Is someone living here?"
*   **Signs of Occupancy**: Cars in driveway, porch furniture, well-kept lawn, seasonal decorations.
*   **Signs of Vacancy**: Overgrown grass (knee-high), pile of yellow phone books, empty interior visible through window, "For Sale" sign.

**The "Bad Dog" Rule**: If you see a dog bowl, assume there is a dog. If you see a "Beware of Dog" sign, believe it.

### Damage Reporting
You are looking for "Asset Risk".
*   **Roof**: Missing shingles? Tarp?
*   **Vandalism**: Graffiti, broken windows?
*   **Disaster**: Flood lines, fire damage?
Report these immediately. A "good" condition report on a house with a hole in the roof is a failed inspection.

### Loss Drafts
These are specialized inspections for insurance claims.
*   **Scenario**: A house had a fire. Insurance paid $50k for repairs. The bank holds the money and releases it in "draws" (chunks) as work is completed.
*   **Your Job**: Verify the % of work done. "Is 50% of the drywall up? Yes/No."
*   **Precision matters**: You need to know the difference between "framed" and "drywalled".

## Chapter 3: Submitting Results

### Photo Requirements
*   **The 6-Angle Rule**: Front, Left Angle, Right Angle, Street View Left, Street View Right, House Number.
*   **Date/Time Stamp**: ESSENTIAL. Most apps add this automatically.
*   **Clarity**: No thumb obstruction, no blurry windshield shots. Get out of the car.

### Choosing Your Words
*   **Objective**: "Siding is discolored" (Fact).
*   **Subjective**: "Siding looks ugly and old" (Opinion - Avoid this).
*   **Definitive**: "Property is vacant" (Fact, supported by evidence).

### Handling Rejections
If a coordinator rejects your report, it's usually because:
1.  **Missing Photo**: You claimed "Roof damage" but provided no photo of the roof.
2.  **Blurry Photos**: They can't see the house number.
3.  **Wrong Address**: You inspected the neighbor's house. (It happens. Double-check GPS).
